Archives are not checked. Their type is confirmed, but nothing looks inside them, so they are marked Not checked and everyone you share them with is warned.

A check tells what kind of file it is, not that it is harmless: a real PDF can still hold something malicious.

Left out on purpose: DOC, XLS, PPT and RTF can carry macros or embedded objects, and HTML, SVG, XML, scripts and programs run code when opened. Share them inside an archive if you must.

02 SignedYour private key signs it, so anyone can tell it comes from you.

Its SHA-256 is signed with your ECDSA P-256 private key, which your password unlocks for this upload only. Your key's certificate is issued by this server's certificate authority.

Every visit to the file's page checks the signature, the certificate and whether it was revoked. The signature proof on that page lets anyone check it with OpenSSL, without the app.

03 SealedA key of its own encrypts it, locked to each reader.

A new random AES-256-GCM key encrypts the file. That key is stored only as copies encrypted to the public key of each person allowed to open it (HPKE, X25519): you now, and each person you share it with later.

The server's own keys cannot open it: a download needs your password, which unlocks your copy of the key for that one request.
